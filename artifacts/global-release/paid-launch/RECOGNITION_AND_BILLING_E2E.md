# Recognition and paid-find evidence

No live positive recognition benchmark or real StoreKit purchase was run. The 13+ provider/guardian route and legal snapshot remain unqualified. The retained OpenAI adapter is not enabled merely because Terms acceptance or the paid ledger is implemented. Actual place accuracy, wrong autonomous saves, multi-place completeness, latency and full-path cost remain unmeasured for this candidate.

The database suite in `paid-find-database-tests.json` exercises real isolated PostgreSQL transactions with synthetic eligibility and verified-store fixtures. It covers one durable source with multiple saved destinations counting once, free-first funding after active unlimited Plus, token reservation/settlement, concurrent free admission and token completion, duplicate delivery, failures/cancellation, pending Quick Check, cached/tutorial exemptions, identity/consent withdrawal, refunds/reversals, recovery, legacy obligations and authorized ownership transfer. Its result is financial logic evidence, not a live provider or Apple receipt.

Qualification runs 101 successful Plus jobs without a numeric quota and leaves purchased tokens intact. Ten simultaneous free admissions yield only three holds; twenty simultaneous token-funded completions consume exactly twenty. The exact final suite count and case names are in the JSON receipt, not inferred from these examples.

Billing is tied to the persisted, owned destination/source and durable share-job identity. The terminal job transition and its financial settlement share a PostgreSQL transaction. The Edge finalizer now propagates rejected database transitions instead of silently treating them as already-completed retries. The ledger releases holds after cancellation/revocation; that behavior alone does not authorize a late automatic save or another provider request. Existing pipeline retry/consent/identity suites are retained.

The independent integration review on 2026-10-29 found that a stale Alice job screen could run billing preflight for a newly signed-in Bob, then save its candidate into Bob's map before job resolution rejected Alice's job. Preflight now reads the owning job under the captured account (and the owning result first for alternative promotion), checks that identity again, and returns the expected owner. The candidate save passes that owner into the real saved-place service's `getUser` boundary; a switch before persistence is rejected. A late response after an account switch cannot seed the newly signed-in account's visible candidate-save cache. Job resolution, named-lead completion and promotion also preflight their specific job/result. Ordinary manual saves without a job retain their existing behavior.

`scripts/testRecognitionBillingPreflight.ts` passed **15 offline runtime boundary checks**. These exercise the real preflight and candidate-save orchestration; the post-preflight account-switch regression invokes the actual `savedPlacesService.saveSavedPlace` authentication boundary with inert dependencies and fails on any unexpected database persistence. Cases cover a stale Alice job under Bob, switches during job lookup/status verification and at save authentication, late-response cache suppression, anonymous ownership, missing authentication, database/status errors, reserved-capacity handling, promotion ownership and the no-job manual path. The log and source hashes are in `tests/recognition-billing-preflight.log` and `tests/recognition-billing-preflight-receipt.json`. These are mocked database/network boundaries, not a live device/account-switch run.

The same review found that the older automatic named-lead RPC could persist a save after consent revocation because settlement released its hold without rejecting the save. The completed repair is migration 00004 (SHA256 e5d1376dc5f70c3650e6e1fa36b140c388a1cfe72870b22fc2eb96124de9349a), deployed to disabled Development. It locks current policy/consent through the original confirmation RPC and guards each service-role saved/source/result/successful-terminal write. An insert guard serializes initially absent legacy revocation rows too. Its separate tests/recognition-commit-authorization.json records 24 passing real PostgreSQL scenarios, including observed lock waits in both commit orders, denied late saves and preserved manual edits/transfers. The 15 client tests do not prove that database repair.

The user-confirmed candidate path still persists the place/source and resolves the job in separate requests. If saving succeeds but resolution fails, the owned save can remain while the job remains unresolved; a retry must reconcile the existing save. The account-owner fix does not make the complete manual save-plus-resolution sequence atomic, and this report does not claim that it does. Automatic RPC save paths and terminal billing transactions have different transaction boundaries.

Still required after actual provider permission and catalog qualification: a bounded authorized live canary, the retained multi-place/no-place/Quick Check dataset, exact destination and source-association review, measured cost and latency, plus integrated Apple purchase→recognition→single settlement journeys. Provider timeout and lost-response tests must include the actual deployed path; offline failure fixtures alone do not close that boundary.
