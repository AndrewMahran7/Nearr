# V2 transfer design — HOLD, not implementation-approved

No V2 migration was created or deployed. The Development migration `20260928000001_onboarding_real_saved_place_transfer.sql` must not be promoted: its duplicate-place path deletes a saved-place row without complete child reconciliation, and its nonduplicate move misses source-owner coherence. Production's unique `(user_id, place_id)` constraint and `saved_place_sources` owner trigger make a simple row-owner update or delete unsafe.

The backup restore exposed **one pre-existing Production source/save owner mismatch** (also confirmed live by a read-only count). Thus the necessary invariant is already false for one row. Before coding a transfer RPC, determine the origin and product meaning of that row with authorized, privacy-preserving inspection, agree a forward repair, and retest its effects on old-client source links and RLS. Do not silently normalize or delete it as part of an account transfer.

Proposed semantics for review: authorize source and destination through an expiring, destination-bound grant; lock the grant and affected owner/place/source rows in one transaction; exclude local-only tutorial fixtures; keep destination saved-place identity when its Google Place ID duplicates a source save; preserve destination notes/reminders unless product rules explicitly say otherwise; reconcile eligible source associations and dependent queue/result/notification references without cross-account leakage; make retries return the committed outcome. A duplicate source identity must become one association, not two. A unique save must move with all owned children coherently. Any unreconciled dependent reference must abort the entire transaction. The exact behavior for historical cross-owner sources, notification history, pending share jobs, and conflicting notes/reminders is **not yet approved**.

Until those semantics and real PostgreSQL adversarial/concurrent tests pass, the only safe migration is the already-staged nullable provider-name migration, and even that is held by the whole-release gates.
